Signature Piece

The Floating Block

A single deep block of concrete that looks like it was poured where it stands — and then, impossibly, lifted an inch off the floor.

The trick is in what you can't see. The base is set well inside the footprint of the top, so from any seated angle the plinth disappears into its own shadow and the block reads as solid mass hovering over the rug. It's the piece people walk around twice before they say anything.

The surface is where the character lives. Trowelled in thin layers by hand, it dries to a mottled storm-grey — lighter across the top where the light falls, darkening down the sides, with the faint cloud-like variation that only comes from a hand-worked finish. Run your palm across it and it's smooth as riverstone, with just enough tooth to tell you it's real.

It's a big, confident piece and it wants a big room. In the home above it sits against a deep velvet sofa in a high-ceilinged Victorian front room, and it holds its own against the cornicing and the log burner without shouting.

In the Workshop

Five weeks, mostly by hand

There's no mould and no production line. Here's what actually happens between your measurements and your living room.

01

Your measurements

We start with the room, not the table. Sofa length, walkways, doorways, the height of your seat cushions — all of it decides the final dimensions. Send photos and we'll tell you honestly if a size will feel wrong.

02

The core

A rigid, dimensionally stable core is built to those exact measurements. This is what makes a piece this size liftable by two people instead of needing a crane — and it's why the concrete won't crack.

03

Trowelling

Concrete goes on in thin layers by hand, each one left to cure before the next. The tonal movement across the surface is the record of that process — which is why no two pieces have ever matched.

04

Burnish & seal

The surface is burnished smooth, then given a penetrating sealer and a matt topcoat. Wine, coffee and a hot mug straight from the kettle are all fine. Then we deliver it and put it where you want it.

Before You Order

The questions we're always asked

Is it solid concrete? It looks incredibly heavy.
It's real concrete, hand-trowelled in layers over a rigid core rather than poured as one solid lump. That's deliberate: a genuinely solid block this size would be several hundred kilos and would need specialist lifting. Ours are heavy — you'll want a second person — but they can be carried in through a normal front door and moved again later if you rearrange.
Will it stain if someone puts a wine glass down?
No. Every piece gets a penetrating sealer followed by a matt topcoat, which is the same approach used on concrete kitchen worktops. Red wine, coffee, oil and a hot mug straight from the kettle are all fine. Wipe spills up when you notice them rather than leaving them overnight and it'll look the same in ten years.
How do I clean it?
A damp cloth, and washing-up liquid if it needs it. The only things to avoid are bleach, limescale removers and anything abrasive, since those attack the sealer rather than the concrete. Re-sealing is a twenty-minute job every few years and we'll send you the kit and instructions free of charge.
Can I get the exact colour in the photographs?
Very close, but not identical — and that's the nature of a hand-trowelled finish. We match the base colour precisely, but the tonal movement across the surface is created by hand on the day and has never repeated. If you need a specific shade to work with an existing floor or worktop, send a photo or a sample and we'll match to it.
How long will I wait?
Four to six weeks for coffee tables, six to eight for dining tables, counted from the point your dimensions are signed off rather than from your order. Each layer of concrete has to cure before the next goes on, and that part can't be rushed. Homeware ships within 5–7 days.
How does it get to me?
We deliver furniture ourselves across the North East and bring it into the room you want it in — no kerbside drop-offs. Elsewhere in mainland UK we arrange a two-person delivery service; tell us your postcode and we'll confirm the cost before you commit. Homeware goes by tracked courier, free over £40.
What if it doesn't fit?
We'd rather catch that before it's built, so we always ask about doorways, stairs and tight turns for anything over a metre. Made-to-order furniture is exempt from standard distance-selling returns because it can't be resold, which is exactly why we spend time on measurements first. Homeware has a straightforward 30-day return.
